Module 00

Before You Start

Two things to do before touching any tool. Skip this module and you'll rebuild things you've already set up. Do it and you'll know exactly what you're keeping, what you're replacing, and what you're throwing away.

The subscription audit: what are you actually paying for?

Before adding anything, you need a complete picture of what you're currently paying. Most people are surprised by how much they find when they actually look.

Open your bank or credit card statement and filter for the last 60 days. Write down every recurring software or app charge: name, amount, and billing frequency. Don't skip anything. That $6.99/month note-taking app counts.
For each item, answer honestly: Did I use this more than 3 times in the last 30 days? Not "do I intend to use it" or "it might be useful." Did you actually open it and use it. Circle the ones where the answer is no.
Of the ones you do use, ask: does another tool I'm already paying for do this? Email clients, note apps, and project management tools are the worst offenders. You may be paying for three things that do the same job.
Don't cancel anything yet. By the end of this guide you'll know exactly what to replace with something free. Cancel then, with a clear alternative already set up. Not now, when you'd just be cutting something without a replacement.
Annual subscriptions are especially easy to forget. Check your email for receipts with "annual renewal" or "yearly plan" in the subject. These hit once a year and disappear from your mental accounting, but they add up fast.
What you're building by the end of this guide
AI Assistant
One primary tool for writing, research, and daily questions. Configured and used with intention. Not a dozen tabs open at once.
Email Marketing
Brevo set up with your domain, a working welcome sequence, and a contact form connected to your list.
Automation
At least one Make scenario running: a real workflow that removes a task you currently do by hand.
CRM
HubSpot configured for your specific business, with your pipeline stages, contact properties, and at least 5 real contacts imported.
Workspace
Notion set up as your actual operating system. Not a blank page. SOPs, a client tracker, and a weekly task view.
Scheduling
TidyCal or Calendly live and embedded on your site or in your email signature, so clients can book without emailing back and forth.
The goal is not more tools. The goal is fewer, better-configured tools that actually run in the background while you work. Everything in this guide either costs nothing or has a one-time price. No subscription treadmill.
Module 01

Your AI Assistant

Most people use writing assistants wrong. They open a new chat for every question, never give context, and wonder why the output feels generic. This module covers how to set up Claude as a proper business tool, not a novelty, including how to give it your brand voice so you stop rewriting everything it produces.

Why one primary AI, not three

Every major writing assistant, Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, will tell you it's the best at everything. They're all wrong. Each has real strengths. But the productivity trap is using all of them simultaneously, because you spend more time deciding which tool to open than actually using any of them.

Pick one and go deep on it. Learn its patterns, its limits, and how to prompt it well. You will get dramatically better results from one well-used tool than from three used casually.

The free tier rule: Both Claude and ChatGPT have free tiers that are genuinely useful, not crippled versions. Run the free tier of your primary tool for 30 days before paying. You'll know by then whether the daily message limits are actually blocking you.
Setting up Claude for your business

Claude's Projects feature (available on free and Pro tiers) lets you store persistent context, your business description, writing style, and instructions, so you don't re-explain yourself every conversation.

Go to claude.ai and create a free account. Use the email you check daily. Claude sends important security and account notifications there.
In the left sidebar, click Projects → New Project. Name it after your business. This is a persistent workspace where Claude remembers your context across every conversation inside it.
Click Project Instructions and write a description of your business. Include: what you do, who your clients are, your tone of voice, and any words or phrases you always use or never use. This is what makes AI output actually sound like you.
Upload any reference documents you want Claude to know: a services list, your pricing, a sample of your best writing, your FAQ. Claude will reference these without you having to paste them every time.
Context is everything. "Write me an email about my new service" produces generic output. "Write an email to an existing client who last booked three months ago, announcing my new monthly retainer option. Keep it under 150 words, conversational, no corporate language" produces something usable. The more specific you are, the less you rewrite.
A sample project instruction that works: "You are helping run [Business Name], a [type of business] based in [location] serving [client type]. My writing tone is [direct / warm / professional / casual, pick yours]. I never use jargon, buzzwords, or phrases like 'leverage', 'synergy', or 'circle back'. When I ask you to write something, give me one version, not three options. If something I ask is unclear, ask one clarifying question before writing."
Tasks AI actually saves time on

Not everything benefits from AI. The tasks where it genuinely saves hours are specific.

First drafts of anything: Emails, proposals, social captions, FAQ answers, service descriptions. Get a rough draft in 30 seconds and edit from there. Starting from a blank page is the expensive part. A good writing tool eliminates it.
Repurposing content: Turn a long email into 3 social posts. Turn a client FAQ conversation into a blog draft. Turn a service description into a DM script. One piece of content, multiple formats, 10 minutes instead of 2 hours.
Explaining things you don't understand: DNS records, automation logic, contract terms, tax forms. Ask Claude to explain anything in plain English, at the level of detail you need. This guide does this. Any term you don't recognize, ask.
Editing and proofreading: Paste anything you've written and ask: "Make this clearer. Keep my voice. Don't change the meaning." More useful than Grammarly for anything longer than a paragraph.
Creating templates: Ask once: "Create a client onboarding email template for my business that I can fill in with the client's name, service, and start date." Save the output. Use it 100 times.
Tasks where AI wastes your time

Knowing where AI underperforms saves you from the frustrating loop of re-prompting and rewriting output that was never going to be right.

Don't use AI for
Decisions that require your actual business judgment. Pricing your services. Deciding which client to take on. Reading a contract for legal risk. These tools can inform the decision, but they cannot make it for you.
Don't trust AI for
Specific facts, current prices, live data, or legal/tax specifics. Always verify anything with legal or financial consequences with a real source. AI can be wrong with complete confidence.
Common Issues
AI output sounds generic and not like me
This is a prompting problem, not a model problem. You haven't given enough voice context. Go back to your Project Instructions and add 3 to 5 examples of your actual writing. The more sample text Claude has, the better it mirrors your style. You can also add: "Here is an example of writing I like: [paste a real email you wrote]."
Claude hits its daily message limit before I'm done
This means you're using it enough that the free tier is genuinely limiting your work, which is exactly the right signal to upgrade to Pro ($20/mo). Before doing so, batch your tasks. Instead of 10 separate conversations, write everything you need in one session. Also consider whether some tasks could be done with ChatGPT's free tier while Claude resets.
I asked Claude something and the answer seems wrong
Trust your instinct. Claude can be confidently incorrect, especially on specific facts, recent events, or niche technical details. Always verify anything with real consequences. For factual questions, ask Claude to tell you where you could verify the information rather than just taking the answer at face value.
Module 02

Email Marketing with Brevo

Brevo gives you the most generous free tier in email marketing: unlimited contacts, 300 emails per day, and transactional email included. It takes about an hour to go from account creation to your first campaign live. This module covers everything: account setup, domain verification, your first list, a welcome sequence, and connecting your contact form.

Creating your Brevo account
Go to brevo.com and click "Sign up free." Use your business email address, not a personal Gmail, because you'll be sending from this domain and it needs to match your sending domain setup later.
Verify your email. You'll land on the Brevo dashboard. Before doing anything else, go to My Profile → Plan & Billing and confirm you're on the Free plan. Don't click any upgrade prompts yet - the free tier is where you start.
Go to Senders & IP → Senders and add your sending address. This is the "From" address your subscribers will see. Use something like hello@yourdomain.com or name@yourdomain.com - not a free email provider.
Do not skip domain verification. Sending from an unverified domain gets your emails routed to spam. It will look like Brevo isn't working when the real problem is deliverability. Domain verification takes 10 to 15 minutes and completely solves this.
Verifying your sending domain

Domain verification proves to email servers worldwide that you're authorized to send email from your domain. Without it, your emails hit spam. It involves adding three DNS records to your domain. It sounds technical, but it's adding three rows to a table in your domain registrar's control panel.

In Brevo, go to Senders & IP → Domains → Add a domain. Enter your domain name (e.g., yourbusiness.com) and click Authenticate.
Brevo shows you three DNS records to add: a DKIM record (proves emails came from you), an SPF record (lists servers authorized to send from your domain), and a DMARC record (tells receiving servers what to do with emails that fail those checks). Copy all three.
Log into wherever you registered your domain: GoDaddy, Namecheap, Google Domains, Cloudflare, etc. Find the DNS settings (sometimes called DNS Management, DNS Records, or Advanced DNS). Add each record Brevo gave you. The "Name" field is the host, the "Value" field is the content.
Return to Brevo and click Verify & Authenticate. DNS changes typically propagate within 15 to 30 minutes. If Brevo says it can't verify yet, wait an hour and try again.
Common Issues
Brevo says domain is unverified after I added the records
DNS propagation is not instant. Wait at least 30 minutes before retrying. If it's still failing after 2 hours, check that you pasted the record values exactly. Extra spaces or missing characters will cause verification to fail silently. Some registrars also add your domain name automatically to the "Name" field, resulting in a duplicate like brevo._domainkey.yourdomain.com.yourdomain.com. If your registrar does this, enter only the subdomain part in the Name field.
I already have an SPF record for Google Workspace / another provider
You can only have one SPF record per domain. Don't add a second one. Instead, merge them. If your existing record is v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ~all, change it to v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com include:spf.brevo.com ~all. Ask Claude to help you merge your specific records if you're unsure.
I don't have a custom domain, I'm using Gmail
Brevo requires a custom domain for sending. If you don't have one yet, register one. Domains cost about $12/year on Namecheap or Google Domains. This is one of the few costs in this guide that isn't free and isn't optional. A custom domain also makes every email you send look significantly more professional.
AI prompt that explains any DNS issue: "I'm trying to add DNS records to my domain registrar to verify it with Brevo. Here is the record Brevo gave me: [paste record]. Here is what I see in my registrar's DNS panel: [describe the fields]. Tell me exactly what to type in each field." This works for any registrar.
Creating your first contact list

A list in Brevo is a group of contacts. You'll need at least one before you can send a campaign or set up automation. Create separate lists for different types of contacts, current clients, leads, newsletter subscribers, so you can send relevant emails to each group.

Go to Contacts → Lists → Create a list. Name it clearly: "Newsletter Subscribers," "Current Clients," or "Leads." Avoid vague names like "List 1" - you'll have several lists eventually and won't remember what each one is.
Import any contacts you already have. Go to Contacts → Import contacts. You can upload a CSV file from your current email client, CRM, or just a spreadsheet. Minimum columns needed: email address. Optional but useful: first name, last name, phone.
Map your CSV columns to Brevo's contact attributes during import. First name maps to FIRSTNAME, last name to LASTNAME. If you have custom data (e.g., "Service Type"), create a custom attribute under Contacts → Contact Attributes first, then it'll appear in the mapping step.
Only import contacts who have given you permission to email them. Importing a purchased list, a scraped list, or contacts who never opted in will get your account suspended and your domain blacklisted. This is not a Brevo policy technicality. It's how email deliverability works globally. Only import people who have specifically agreed to receive email from you.
Building a welcome sequence

A welcome sequence is a series of automated emails that go out to every new subscriber, triggered by them joining your list. It's the highest-impact automation you can build because it goes to people at the exact moment they're most interested in what you do.

Go to Automation → Create a workflow. Select "Welcome message" as the starting template. This gives you a single email triggered when a contact is added to a list - the right starting point.
Set the trigger: "Contact is added to list" → select your subscriber list. This automation fires every time someone new joins that list.
Create the first email. Use the drag-and-drop editor to build it. Keep it simple: welcome them, remind them what they signed up for, and tell them what's coming. This is not a sales email. It's a handshake.
Add a second email with a 3-day delay. This one can go deeper. Share something useful, link to your most helpful content, or tell your business story. By email two, they've had time to forget they signed up, so briefly reintroduce yourself.
Add a third email 7 days after signup. This is your soft offer. Introduce your services, a special offer for new subscribers, or a clear call to action. By now they've had two useful emails from you, which earns the ask.
AI prompt for each welcome email: "Write a welcome email for my business: [describe what you do]. This is email [1/2/3] in a welcome sequence. The goal is [introduce myself / share something useful / make a soft offer]. My tone is [conversational/professional]. Keep it under 200 words. No subject line needed. I'll write that separately."
Common Issues
Automation isn't sending when I test it
Check that the workflow is activated. There's a toggle at the top right of the workflow editor. Draft workflows don't send. Also check that your test contact was added to the correct list, not a different list.
Emails are going to spam during testing
If domain verification is complete, the next most common cause is spam trigger words in the email content itself: words like "Free," "Guaranteed," "Act Now," "Limited Time," and especially using ALL CAPS or multiple exclamation marks. Run your subject line through a free spam checker (Mail-tester.com is good) before activating.
The drag-and-drop editor isn't loading
Try a different browser. Brevo's editor occasionally has issues in Safari. Chrome or Firefox work most reliably. If it still doesn't load, clear your browser cache or try an incognito window.
Connecting your contact form

Any contact or signup form on your website should automatically add the person to your Brevo list. There are two ways to do this: Brevo's native embed form, or an API call from your form's submit handler.

Option A - Brevo embed form (simplest): Go to Email Campaigns → Subscription Forms → Create a form. Design it, select the list to subscribe people to, and copy the embed code. Paste it into your website HTML wherever you want the form to appear.
Option B - API from your existing form: If you already have a contact form on your site and want new contacts added to Brevo automatically, use Brevo's Contacts API. Get your API key from My Profile → SMTP & API → API Keys. Then add a fetch call to your form's submit handler.
Test it: submit your own email via the form and verify the contact appears in Brevo under the correct list within 60 seconds. Then check that the welcome sequence fires. You should receive the first welcome email within a few minutes.
// Add to Brevo list when form is submitted async function addToBrevo(email, firstName) { const res = await fetch('https://api.brevo.com/v3/contacts', { method: 'POST', headers: { 'api-key': 'YOUR_BREVO_API_KEY', // use an env variable in production 'Content-Type': 'application/json' }, body: JSON.stringify({ email: email, attributes: { FIRSTNAME: firstName }, listIds: [3], // replace 3 with your actual list ID updateEnabled: true // updates the contact if they already exist }) }); return res.ok; }
Finding your list ID: In Brevo, go to Contacts → Lists. Click on the list you want. The list ID is the number in the URL: app.brevo.com/contact/list/3. Use that number in the listIds array above.
Module 03

Automation with Make

Automation is not a feature. It's time you get back. This module teaches you how Make works, how to build your first real scenario, and the three automations that save most small businesses the most hours. You do not need to know how to code. You do need to think clearly about what triggers what.

How Make works: the mental model

Every automation in Make is a scenario, a sequence of steps where one thing triggers another. There are two types of steps:

Trigger
The event that starts the scenario. Examples: a form is submitted, a new row is added to a spreadsheet, an email arrives with a specific label, a Stripe payment is confirmed.
Action
What happens as a result. Examples: send an email, add a row to a spreadsheet, create a contact in HubSpot, send a Slack message, add a tag in Brevo.

A scenario is always: when [trigger], do [action]. The power comes from chaining multiple actions: when a form is submitted → add contact to Brevo → create contact in HubSpot → send yourself a notification email. Three things done automatically instead of one thing done manually.

Creating your Make account
Go to make.com and sign up free. The free plan gives 1,000 operations per month and 2 active scenarios, enough to build and test everything in this module before deciding whether to upgrade.
When you log in, you land on the Scenarios page. Click Create a new scenario. You'll see a blank canvas with a large plus button in the center. This is where you add your first module (Make calls each step a "module").
Click the plus to add your first module. This is your trigger. In the search box, type the name of the app or service that will trigger the scenario. For your first build, search for Webhooks. This is the most versatile trigger and lets you send data to Make from any form or app.
Your first automation: form submission → email notification

The simplest and most immediately useful automation: when someone submits a contact form on your website, you get an email notification with their details. Currently, you either check your email manually or miss inquiries for hours. This runs in the background forever.

Create a new scenario. Add a Webhooks → Custom webhook trigger. Make generates a unique URL. Copy it - this is the address your website form will send data to.
Click Save, then click the webhook module and hit Run once. Make is now listening for a test submission. Go to your website contact form and submit it with test data (use your own name and email). Make captures the structure of the incoming data.
Now add an action: click the + after the webhook module. Search for Email → Send an email. Fill in the To field (your email), Subject (something like "New inquiry from {{name}}"), and Body (map in the fields from the webhook data - name, email, message).
Turn the scenario on using the toggle at the bottom left of the canvas. Submit the form again on your website. Within seconds you should receive the email notification with the form data filled in.
Once you have the webhook working, you can add more actions to the same scenario. After the email notification, add another module: create a contact in HubSpot with the same data. One form submission → notification email + CRM contact, both automatically. This is where Make earns its place.
Common Issues
Make ran the scenario but no email arrived
Check the scenario execution log by clicking the clock icon at the bottom of the canvas to see recent runs. If the run shows a green checkmark but no email, the email went to spam. Check spam first. If the run shows an error (red icon), click it to see the error message. Common causes: wrong email address in the To field, or the Email module needs you to connect an email account.
The webhook isn't capturing my form data
Your form needs to POST to the webhook URL. Check the form submit handler in your HTML. The fetch call needs the method set to POST and the body needs to be JSON with your form fields. Make sure Content-Type is set to application/json. If you're using a third-party form builder, check whether it supports webhook or Zapier integrations. Most do.
Scenario keeps turning off by itself
This usually means the scenario errored during a live run. Make pauses scenarios after consecutive errors to prevent infinite failure loops. Fix the error (check the execution log for what failed), then manually re-enable the scenario.
I've used up my 1,000 free operations
Each step in a scenario counts as one operation. A 3-step scenario uses 3 operations per run. Upgrade to Core ($9/mo) for 10,000 operations, which covers most small businesses indefinitely. Alternatively, audit which scenarios are running most frequently and consolidate steps where possible.
Three more automations worth building
New client → onboarding email sequence: Trigger: new contact added to a HubSpot list tagged "New Client." Action: send a series of emails via Brevo (welcome, what to expect, how to reach you). Removes the manual step of sending onboarding info every time.
Calendly booking → CRM contact: Trigger: new Calendly booking confirmed. Action: check if contact already exists in HubSpot, create it if not, log the meeting as an activity. Your CRM stays current without any manual data entry after calls.
Weekly digest email to yourself: Trigger: Schedule module, every Monday at 8am. Action: pull stats from your tools (HubSpot contacts added this week, Brevo subscribers gained) and email yourself a summary. Keeps you informed without logging into each platform separately.
Module 04

CRM Setup with HubSpot

HubSpot's free CRM is the most powerful free tool in this entire guide. It also has the highest risk of being set up wrong and abandoned. Most people create an account, import a list, and then never open it again because nothing is configured for how their business actually works. This module is about building a CRM you'll actually use.

What a CRM is actually for

CRM stands for Customer Relationship Management, which sounds corporate and irrelevant. What it actually means: a system that tells you, at any moment, the status of every relationship that matters to your business. Who are your current clients? Who's in conversation but hasn't signed yet? Who went quiet two months ago that you should follow up with?

Without a CRM, this information lives in your head, your inbox, and scattered notes. Which means important things fall through the cracks. Not because you're disorganized, but because you're running everything from memory. The CRM offloads that cognitive load.

Creating your HubSpot account
Go to hubspot.com/crm and click "Get HubSpot free." Create an account with your business email. When it asks about your company size and role, answer honestly. HubSpot uses this to set up your workspace. Don't lie to get a different tier; the free tier is what you want.
HubSpot will offer you a product tour. Take it. It's 5 minutes and shows you where everything is. Skip the upgrade prompts. You'll see orange banners throughout the interface suggesting paid features. Ignore them entirely for now. Everything you need for this module is free.
Go to Settings (gear icon) → Account Defaults and set your company name, currency, time zone, and fiscal year. These settings affect how dates and amounts display across the CRM - getting them right now saves confusion later.
Configuring your pipeline for your actual business

HubSpot's default pipeline stages are: Appointment Scheduled → Qualified to Buy → Presentation Scheduled → Decision Maker Bought-In → Contract Sent → Closed Won / Closed Lost. These stages are designed for a B2B sales team, not a service business. Replace them entirely.

Go to CRM → Deals → Manage pipelines. Click on the default pipeline to edit it. Rename each stage to reflect your actual process. A service business pipeline typically looks more like: Inquiry Received → Proposal Sent → Deposit Paid → Project Active → Complete / Lost.
Delete any stages that don't apply to your workflow. You can always add more later. Four or five meaningful stages beat eight vague ones.
For each stage, set a probability percentage. HubSpot uses this to forecast revenue. Inquiry Received might be 10%, Deposit Paid might be 90%. These don't need to be precise; they just need to reflect rough likelihood.
Build the pipeline for the business you have today, not the business you plan to have. Over-engineering a CRM pipeline before you have consistent deal volume leads to a system that's too complex to maintain. Start simple. You can always add stages.
Importing your existing contacts
Go to Contacts → Import → Start an import → File from computer. Prepare a CSV with at minimum: First Name, Last Name, Email. Additional useful columns: Phone, Company, Notes, Last Service Date.
On the mapping screen, HubSpot guesses which column maps to which property. Verify every mapping. HubSpot often guesses wrong on custom columns. If a column doesn't match a HubSpot property, create a new custom contact property on the fly.
After import, go to Contacts and verify your contacts appear with correct data. Click on one or two to check that all fields populated correctly. It's much faster to fix import errors immediately than after you've started using the CRM.
Create a deal for each active client. Go to Deals → Create deal. Associate the deal with the contact, set the stage (where they are right now in your pipeline), and add any relevant notes. This takes 2 minutes per client and is the foundation everything else is built on.
Common Issues
Contacts imported but deals didn't come with them
Deals and contacts are separate objects in HubSpot. Importing contacts doesn't create deals. You either need to create deals manually (practical for under 20 clients) or use a second import file specifically for deals. Go to Deals → Import to import deals separately, then associate them with contacts during the import mapping step.
HubSpot keeps asking me to connect my email inbox
Email connection is a paid feature. The prompts are aggressive but dismissable. Click "Maybe later" or "Skip" on any inbox connection prompt - you don't need it. You can still log emails manually from a contact's record by clicking Log activity → Email.
Duplicate contacts after import
HubSpot deduplicates by email address. If you have duplicates, the most common cause is contacts with slightly different email formats (extra space, different capitalization). Go to Contacts → Actions → Manage duplicates. HubSpot has a built-in duplicate merging tool that walks you through each set of duplicates and lets you choose which record to keep.
Three HubSpot habits that make the CRM actually useful

A CRM is only as useful as the data in it. These three habits take under 5 minutes a day and are what separates a CRM that tells you things from one that just sits there.

Log every significant conversation. After a call or meeting with a client or prospect, spend 90 seconds writing a note in HubSpot. Not a transcript. Just one or two sentences about what was discussed and what the next step is. This is your memory for future-you.
Move deals through the pipeline in real time. When a proposal goes out, move the deal to "Proposal Sent." When a deposit comes in, move it to "Active." The pipeline is only useful if it reflects reality. A deal stuck in "Inquiry Received" for three months is just noise.
Use the Tasks feature for follow-ups. After any conversation where you said "I'll follow up next week", create a Task in HubSpot assigned to yourself with a due date. When you open HubSpot each morning, your task list tells you exactly who to contact. No more "I meant to follow up with that person" guilt.
Module 05

Your Workspace with Notion

Most people open Notion, see a blank page, and close it. The tool looks intimidating until you understand that it's just a flexible container. It's whatever you build inside it. This module gives you an actual starting structure for a solo service business, not a blank slate.

What Notion replaces

Before setting up Notion, audit what you're currently using for internal work. Notion realistically replaces all of these, but only if you actually migrate your content to it rather than running both in parallel.

Replaces
Google Docs for SOPs and internal documents. Trello or Asana for task tracking. Evernote or Apple Notes for business notes. Airtable or spreadsheets for lightweight client tracking. Any to-do app you're paying for.
Does not replace
Your CRM (HubSpot handles client relationships better). Email marketing (Brevo). Dedicated invoicing tools. Anything that needs to be shared publicly with clients. Notion pages can be shared but aren't designed as client-facing portals.
Creating your Notion account and workspace
Go to notion.so and create a free account. Use your business email. When Notion asks what you're using it for, select "For myself". This sets up a personal workspace, not a team workspace. You can add team members later if needed.
Notion starts you with some default pages. Delete them. They're templates for Notion's use case, not yours. Right-click each one in the sidebar and select Delete.
Create four top-level pages to start. In the sidebar, click the + next to "Private" to create a new page. Name them: Operations, Clients, Projects, and Notes. These become the folders that everything else lives inside.
Building your Operations hub

Operations is where your SOPs, templates, and business reference information lives. Every repeatable process in your business should have a written SOP. Not because you'll forget how to do it, but because it forces you to think about the process clearly and makes delegation possible later.

Inside the Operations page, create sub-pages for each major area: Client Onboarding, Service Delivery, Admin & Finance, Templates. You don't need to fill all of these immediately. Create the structure first.
Write your first SOP. Pick the process you do most often, maybe your new client onboarding checklist or your service delivery steps. Write it out as a numbered list in Notion. Don't aim for perfect; aim for documented. A rough SOP beats relying on memory every time.
In the Templates section, create a page for each email or document you send repeatedly: welcome emails, proposal structure, follow-up messages. Paste in your best existing version. Update it when you find a better approach. Over time this becomes your personal playbook.
AI prompt for writing SOPs: "Help me write an SOP for my [process name] process. Here's how I currently do it: [describe in rough terms]. Format it as numbered steps. Flag anywhere the process could be automated or delegated. Keep it practical. It's for my own reference, not for corporate documentation."
Building your client tracker

The Clients page should be a database, not just a list of text. Notion databases let you filter, sort, and view your client information in different ways. A database page shows a different side of the same data depending on how you're looking at it.

Open your Clients page. Type /database and select Database - Full page. This creates a table. Each row is a client. Each column is a property.
Add these columns: Status (select type, options: Active / Paused / Past / Lead), Service (text), Start Date (date), Next Followup (date), Email (email type), Notes (text). Rename the default "Name" column to "Client Name."
Add a filter view. Click + Add a view → Board. Set the grouping to "Status." Now you can see your clients arranged by status - like a visual pipeline. Switch between Table (all data) and Board (visual status) depending on what you need.
Add each current client as a row. Click into each client's row to open their full page. This becomes their internal file. Paste in any relevant notes, documents, or history. This is your client record, separate from HubSpot which tracks the sales and relationship side.
Notion and HubSpot serve different purposes. HubSpot tracks the relationship: conversations, emails, sales activity. Notion stores project delivery: what you're doing for the client, notes from sessions, documents, checklists. They're complementary, not duplicates.
Common Issues
The database is getting messy, rows everywhere with no structure
Add a Status filter to hide Archived and Past rows from the default view. In the database, click Filter → Add a filter → Status → is not → Past/Archived. Save this as the default view. You can always click "Show all" when you need the full history. Databases get hard to use when they show everything at once; filters keep the working view clean.
I can't figure out how to link pages or create sub-items
Type @ anywhere in Notion to link to another page. Type / to see all the block types you can add. For sub-items inside a database row, open the row by clicking the open icon (↗) at the right of the row. The full page that opens can contain any Notion blocks: text, checklists, databases, files, anything.
Module 06

Scheduling with TidyCal

The back-and-forth of scheduling, "Does Tuesday work?" "Tuesday's busy, how about Thursday?" "Thursday afternoon?", is the most expensive free activity in most small businesses. A scheduling link eliminates it completely. This module gets TidyCal set up and embedded where clients can actually find and use it.

Why TidyCal over Calendly

Both tools work well. The reason this guide recommends TidyCal is simple: it charges $29 once rather than $10 to $16 every month. Run the numbers: $29 vs $120/year. TidyCal pays for itself in three months and then costs nothing forever. The feature set is comparable for most service businesses. If you already have Calendly and the free tier covers your needs, you don't need to switch.

If you have a complex multi-staff scheduling setup or need features specific to Calendly's paid tiers (like round-robin routing or Salesforce integration), Calendly may be worth its price for your specific use case. The $29 TidyCal lifetime deal is the better choice for most solo service businesses.
Setting up TidyCal
Go to tidycal.com and create a free account. The free tier is worth starting with to verify the tool works for your needs before paying. The $29 lifetime upgrade is available anytime from your account settings.
Connect your calendar. TidyCal works with Google Calendar, Outlook, and Apple Calendar. Go to Integrations → Calendar connections and connect your primary calendar. TidyCal reads your existing events to know when you're busy - it never adds or modifies events in your calendar without your permission.
Set your availability. Go to Availability and define the days and hours you're open to bookings. Be realistic. Set times you'll actually show up for, not aspirational availability. Leave buffer time between appointments; TidyCal doesn't add automatic buffers unless you configure them.
Create your first booking type. Go to Booking types → New booking type. Name it, set the duration, add a description, and configure the confirmation and reminder emails. If you're on the paid plan, you can add payment via Stripe so clients pay when they book.
Common Issues
My calendar is connected but TidyCal shows all times as available
TidyCal reads "busy" events from your calendar. Events marked as "Free" in Google Calendar won't block time in TidyCal. Open Google Calendar, click on events you want to block, and change their status from "Free" to "Busy." Also check that TidyCal is reading from the correct calendar if you have multiple calendars on the same account.
Clients can book times outside my working hours
Go back to Availability settings and double-check your hours for each day. TidyCal can sometimes reset availability when you first connect a calendar. Also verify the timezone in your TidyCal settings matches your actual timezone. A mismatch shows the wrong times to clients and creates confusion about when appointments actually are.
Confirmation emails aren't arriving after booking
Check the booking type settings - confirmation emails are off by default in some configurations. Go to your booking type → Edit → Email notifications and make sure both "Confirmation to booker" and "Notification to me" are enabled. Also check your spam folder for the first few bookings to verify deliverability.
Putting your booking link where clients can find it

A scheduling tool no one can find doesn't help anyone. Your booking link should be visible everywhere a potential client might be looking for you.

Email signature: Add a line to your email signature: "Book a call: [your TidyCal link]". This is the highest-impact placement. Every email you send becomes a passive invitation to schedule.
Website: Add a "Book Now" button anywhere on your site that links directly to your TidyCal booking page. If you want it embedded (so clients don't leave your site), use TidyCal's embed code under Booking type → Share → Embed.
Social media bios: Add the booking link to your Instagram bio, LinkedIn profile, and any other platform where potential clients discover you. One link that does one thing: books an appointment.
After a cold outreach: When you send a follow-up message or DM, end with: "Happy to jump on a quick call. Here's a link to grab time: [link]." Removes the scheduling friction from the most friction-heavy moment in the sales process.
Module 07

The Cancellation Audit

You've now built everything you need. This module is the cleanup. Go through every subscription you identified in Module 0 and deciding, with a clear alternative in place, what to cancel, what to downgrade, and what to keep.

The decision framework

For each subscription, there are only four possible outcomes. Apply them in order:

Cancel immediately: You didn't use it more than 3 times last month, and this guide provides a free alternative that's now set up. There is no reason to keep paying. Cancel today, not "soon."
Downgrade to free: You use the tool, but only features that exist on the free tier. Log into the account, find the plan settings, and downgrade. You lose nothing except the monthly charge.
Keep and justify: You actively use paid features that genuinely save you time or generate revenue. Write down the specific feature and why it earns its cost. If you can't name the feature, that's a signal to reconsider.
Set a 30-day review: You're not sure. Keep it for 30 days but set a calendar reminder to decide. Uncertainty is not a reason to keep paying indefinitely. It's a reason to gather more data.

Before cancelling email or CRM platforms: Always export your data first. Every platform has an export function - usually under Settings → Data or Account → Export. Export before you cancel, not after. Once you cancel, you may lose access to your data immediately.
How to migrate from Mailchimp to Brevo

This is the most common migration, so it gets its own walkthrough.

In Mailchimp, go to Audience → All contacts → Export audience. Download the CSV. Open it and verify it contains email, first name, last name, and any custom fields you care about.
If you have multiple Mailchimp lists (audiences), export each one separately. Name each CSV by the list it came from. You'll import them into separate Brevo lists.
Export your automations, not the contacts, but the email content. In each automation, copy the email content somewhere (a Notion page works). You'll rebuild these in Brevo. Having the original content saves rewriting from scratch.
In Brevo, import each CSV to its corresponding list (Module 2 covered this). Rebuild your welcome sequence using the copied content. Test with your own email before activating.
Run both platforms in parallel for 2 weeks. Send campaigns from Brevo only, but don't cancel Mailchimp yet. Verify deliverability, open rates, and that automations are firing correctly. After 2 weeks with no issues, cancel Mailchimp.
